Result

  • Map value: 333 days.
  • Observer B (full map) could act on event E01, public on 2024-07-01, 333 days before the first production stop on 2025-05-30.
  • Observer A (public map, named links) had no connected signal early enough to act. Its first connected signal of any kind was E04, 2 days before the stop.
  • Needed lead time in the grid: 3 to 166 days.
  • Variant with generic links: map value 0 days.

The case

On 4 April 2025, China put export licensing on seven medium and heavy rare earths, including dysprosium- and terbium-containing magnet materials. These magnets sit in electric motors, actuators and sensors in vehicles. Licences came slowly, magnet exports fell, and in late May and early June 2025 vehicle makers and suppliers reported stops: Ford in Chicago, Suzuki in Japan and several supplier plants in Europe. The hidden node here is not one firm but a country-level supply of a material.

The question

Two observers watch the same public signals. Observer A knows only the links that a public source names, firm by firm, on each date. Observer B knows the full map from the start, as a multi-tier tool or supplier compliance data could give a firm. The map value is the lead time of B minus the lead time of A. The case also counts false alarms: how often a Chinese export control on a material was followed by a vehicle production stop.

The rules of this case were committed before any evidence was collected (case/rare-earths/PREREGISTRATION.md).

Method

  • Point-in-time evidence. Every link and every event has a source with a date, and a quote of 30 words or fewer that a program finds word for word in the downloaded text (htw/checks.py). Most sources are pinned to an exact copy in the Wayback Machine.
  • Source rule. Evidence comes from primary or official sources (regulators, courts, company documents, official statistics) or, where none exists, from established secondary sources (wire services, trade press, industry associations). Wikipedia, blogs and aggregators are never evidence; the check rejects them. Wikipedia page views are used only as a measure of public attention.
  • Public date. A signal counts from the first day an outsider could know it, which can be later than the day it took effect.
  • First stop. The earliest public report of an actual production stop at a vehicle maker or a tier-1 supplier that the source links to the case. A threat of a stop does not count.
  • Time to act. Each case has a grid of the time to survive (how long stocks last) and the time to recover (how long a new source or a licence takes), with a source quote for each value in case/<case>/assumptions.csv. A signal is actionable in a grid cell if its lead time is at least the time to recover minus the time to survive. The values are an assumption of this study.
  • Connection. An observer can connect a signal if a chain of known links joins it to the vehicle makers. Observer A counts a link only when a source names a specific firm, or the suppliers of a specific firm, on it. The results also show a generic variant, which counts statements about an industry or a country.
  • Window. Signals count from 730 days before the first stop; the results also show 365 and 1095 days.
  • Attention. Daily Wikipedia page views. An attention signal needs three or more spike days in a row. This rule was added after seeing the case-1 data, because single spike days were not followed by any event.
  • False alarms (case 2). For a class of similar signals, the share that was followed by a stop, with a logged search for each signal (case/rare-earths/search_log.csv).
  • Human check. Nikhil Sehgal checked all map links and a random sample of events of each case against the sources before the analysis (case/<case>/gold_check.csv).

Reading of the author

This is interpretation, not a proven finding.

  1. As in the Nexperia case, the decisive early signal was a state measure, not a market signal. Here it was China's rare-earth regulation of 2024, which put exports under state tracing.
  2. The generic dependency was public for years: government reports said that China controls most rare-earth separation and that magnets go into many vehicle parts. Which suppliers and parts depended on it became public only after the first stop. A generic statement did not help an outside observer to act.
  3. Unlike in the Nexperia case, the trigger itself left some time to act, but only where a licence could come quickly. Firms responded with stockpiling; Daimler Truck said it would increase its inventories in the short term.
  4. Most Chinese export controls on materials from 2023 to 2025 were not followed by a vehicle production stop. A firm that acts on every such measure would mostly act on false alarms. The value of a map lies in telling which measure touches its own parts.

Limits

  • Chosen after the fact. This case was also chosen because a disruption happened. The false-alarm class corrects this only for one kind of signal.
  • "No stop found" is not "no stop". The false-alarm test rests on a logged search of public sources. A stop that no public source reported is not counted.
  • Several stops, one date. Reports of the first stops came within a week; the first public report is used.
  • The time-to-act grid is an assumption based on licence review times and statements about stocks.
  • Attention depends on the article. "Dysprosium" reacted within days; "Rare-earth element" did not react until the October 2025 measures. Both articles were chosen after a probe, which the pre-registration says.
  • No firm-specific claim beyond what the cited sources say. Daimler Truck is named only for its own public statement about inventories.
