The 2025 Nexperia chip disruption

How many days of warning was a hidden-tier map worth in the 2025 Nexperia disruption?

Result

  • Map value: 330 days.
  • Observer B (full map) could act on event E01, public on 2024-12-02, 330 days before the first production stop on 2025-10-28.
  • Observer A (public map, named links) had no connected signal early enough to act. Its first connected signal of any kind was E07, 12 days before the stop.
  • Needed lead time in the grid: 48 to 166 days.
  • Variant with generic links: map value 0 days.

The case

Nexperia makes standard chips that tier-1 suppliers build into the modules they sell to vehicle makers. In autumn 2025 a dispute between the Netherlands and China over Nexperia, a subsidiary of the Chinese firm Wingtech, stopped deliveries from Nexperia's China plant. Vehicle makers learned that they depended on a supplier that few of them could see in their own supply chain. Honda stopped vehicle production in Mexico on 28 October 2025.

The question

Two observers watch the same public signals.

  • Observer A knows only the links of the supply chain that a public source named on each date.
  • Observer B knows all links from the start. A multi-tier mapping tool or supplier compliance data could give a firm this map.

For each observer, the study finds the first public signal that the observer could connect to its own supply chain early enough to act. The map value is the lead time of B minus the lead time of A, in days.

Method

  • Point-in-time evidence. Every link and every event has a source with a date, and a quote of 30 words or fewer that a program finds word for word in the downloaded text (htw/checks.py). Most sources are pinned to an exact copy in the Wayback Machine.
  • Source rule. Evidence comes from primary or official sources (regulators, courts, company documents, official statistics) or, where none exists, from established secondary sources (wire services, trade press, industry associations). Wikipedia, blogs and aggregators are never evidence; the check rejects them. Wikipedia page views are used only as a measure of public attention.
  • Public date. A signal counts from the first day an outsider could know it, which can be later than the day it took effect.
  • First stop. The earliest public report of an actual production stop at a vehicle maker or a tier-1 supplier that the source links to the case. A threat of a stop does not count.
  • Time to act. Each case has a grid of the time to survive (how long stocks last) and the time to recover (how long a new source or a licence takes), with a source quote for each value in case/<case>/assumptions.csv. A signal is actionable in a grid cell if its lead time is at least the time to recover minus the time to survive. The values are an assumption of this study.
  • Connection. An observer can connect a signal if a chain of known links joins it to the vehicle makers. Observer A counts a link only when a source names a specific firm, or the suppliers of a specific firm, on it. The results also show a generic variant, which counts statements about an industry or a country.
  • Window. Signals count from 730 days before the first stop; the results also show 365 and 1095 days.
  • Attention. Daily Wikipedia page views. An attention signal needs three or more spike days in a row. This rule was added after seeing the case-1 data, because single spike days were not followed by any event.
  • False alarms (case 2). For a class of similar signals, the share that was followed by a stop, with a logged search for each signal (case/rare-earths/search_log.csv).
  • Human check. Nikhil Sehgal checked all map links and a random sample of events of each case against the sources before the analysis (case/<case>/gold_check.csv).

Reading of the author

This is interpretation, not a proven finding.

  1. In this case, the decisive early signal did not concern the chip maker itself. It concerned its owner, two steps away from the vehicle makers' parts.
  2. Public data showed that the automotive industry depended on Nexperia. It did not show which suppliers, modules or parts did. That gap, not a lack of warnings, is what made the warning useless to an outside observer.
  3. Public attention came late. It rose sharply only when the Dutch order became public, shortly before the first stop. Earlier single spikes were false alarms.
  4. For a firm, the value of a multi-tier map lies in connecting outside signals to its own parts. This one case cannot say how often such signals are false alarms.

Limits

  • One case, chosen after the fact. The case was chosen because a disruption happened, so lead times look better than a live monitor would see. One case gives no false-alarm rate.
  • The December 2024 listing did not apply to Nexperia itself. It was a signal on the owner. The first US rule that covered Nexperia came on 29 September 2025.
  • Public sources found by one person. An earlier public source may exist for a link. The study found the Dongguan plant named only in sources after the crisis.
  • The time-to-act grid is an assumption based on one statement by the vehicle makers' association.
  • Observer B is an ideal. Real multi-tier maps are incomplete, and a firm still has to read and act on a signal.
  • No firm-specific claim. The study makes no claim about the suppliers or the exposure of any named vehicle maker other than what the cited sources say.
